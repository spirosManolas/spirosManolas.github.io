How can mathematics model complex physical systems? My academic career has been primarily motivated by me seeking new and interesting ways to answer this question! To be a little more specific, I am particulalry interested in the development and application of both theoretical (eg asymptotics) and computational (eg numerical methods for PDEs & numerical optimization methods) approaches to answer mathematical modeling questions involving fluid and/or solid mechanics. Though I've recently become interested in problems involving complex fluids and active/self-organizing matter, I tend to find most applications to be interesting! x 

A little about me: I currently am in the fourth year of my undergraduate degree at [Stony Brook University](https://www.stonybrook.edu/) in Applied and Pure Mathematics with a minor in Mechanical Engineering as a [Barry Goldwater Scholar](https://news.stonybrook.edu/university/three-stony-brook-students-turn-research-promise-into-goldwater-recognition/) and [Simons STEM Scholar](https://www.stonybrook.edu/simonsscholars/). I am currently working under Prof. Shikui Chen in the [Computational Modeling Analysis Design and Optimization (CMADO) Laboratory](http://me.eng.stonybrook.edu/~chen/), where my research focuses on developing a novel non-linear topology optimization algorithm that allows for more precise control over how bistable states occur than do previous methods, with applications in designing brain aneurysm stents.

Last summer, I worked with the [Complex Fluids Group of Dr. Howard Stone](https://stonelab.princeton.edu/) at Princeton University, where I explored Modeling a Falling Slinky. Previously, I've worked in [Los Alamos National Laboratory's X-Computational Physics (XCP) Division](https://www.lanl.gov/engage/organizations/xcp) through the Computational Physics Student Summer Workshop under Dr. Nathaniel Vaughn-Kukura and Dr. Mikhail Shashkov; I participated in the [Emory University REU on Computational Mathematics for Data Science](https://www.math.emory.edu/site/cmds-reuret/projects/2024-oed/) where I was mentored by Dr. Nicole Yang; and I also used to work with Prof. [Hyun-Kyung Lim](https://www.ams.stonybrook.edu/~hyulim/) at Stony Brook, where I explored quantum error mitigation for the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA).

Mentorship and providing opportunities for others is incredibly important to me: I currently serve as President of Stony Brook's [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics (SIAM) Student Chapter](https://www.stonybrook.edu/ceas-undergrad/student-organizations/siam.html) and a peer mentor through the Simons STEM Scholars program.

I enjoy using my free time on various hobbies, such as hiking, bike riding, cooking/baking, trying new foods, listening to music, learning origami, and spending time with loved ones!
